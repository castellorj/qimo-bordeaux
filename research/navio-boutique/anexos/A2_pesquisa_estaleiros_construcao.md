# QIMO – Newbuild boutique cruise yacht (~60 m, 36 cabins, 72 pax): shipyards, benchmarks & costs

Research date: 2026-10-07. Prepared as input for the QIMO business plan (to be written in Portuguese).

**Labels**
- **CONFIRMED**: a public source reports the figure. The URL is given. See the method note below.
- **ESTIMATE**: derived by calculation or judgement. The method is stated.
- **PREMISE**: an assumption or background industry knowledge that was not re-verified in this session.

**FX:** 1 EUR = 1.10 USD (PREMISE, standard rate set by the brief); 1 EUR ≈ 10.0 NOK (PREMISE, used only where the source gives no EUR value).

**Method note / limitations.** In this session, WebFetch was blocked for most trade-press domains (cruiseindustrynews, cruisemapper, bairdmaritime, seatrade-cruise, maritime-executive, pressmare and others). As a result, "CONFIRMED" figures come from search-engine extracts of the cited pages, and the full articles were not read. Before the plan is finalised, open each URL and check the figure. The session's web-search budget also ran out before Turkish and Italian yard pricing could be explored further. Most shipyards do not publish contract prices, and none of the Turkish yards we looked at has published one for a cruise vessel.

**Read-across (ESTIMATE).** At superyacht finish, private yachts cost €30–40k/GT. Commercial small cruise ships cost €7–12k/GT (World Navigator ≈ €7k/GT, Sefu ≈ €10k/GT, Silver Origin ≈ €12k/GT). A 72-pax vessel of ~60–68 m would be about 1,500–2,500 GT; Variety Voyager is 68 m, but its GT was not verified, so this is a PREMISE. If the hull were built to commercial passenger-ship standard with a yacht-grade interior, ~€15–25k/GT gives **€25–55 M**. If it were finished to full superyacht standard at €30k+/GT, the cost would be €50–75 M.

### Per-berth synthesis (ESTIMATE)
- Using all disclosed small-ship prices (rows 1–14), the range is **€255k to €1.75M per berth**. The median of rows 3–13 is about **€570k**.
- **Small ships carry a scale penalty.** Bridge, engine room, safety systems and crew facilities are fixed costs that a 72-pax ship spreads over fewer berths than a 150–200-pax ship. Variety Voyager cost €403k/berth in 2012. Escalated to 2026 at ×1.30–1.45, that is **€525–585k/berth** (≈ €38–42 M). The escalation factor is a PREMISE: euro-area inflation 2012→2026 was roughly +33%, and shipbuilding steel, labour and equipment have risen faster since 2021.

**ESTIMATE for QIMO (10-year horizon)**
- **Accounting:** a 25–30-year life with a 10–15% residual gives a book value of **~65–70% of cost at year 10**.
