**Annual crew cost model** (ESTIMATE / PREMISE)
- Seasonal operation: 26–30 operating weeks (late April to late October), plus about 4 weeks prep and lay-up.
- Core year-round (Master, Chief Eng, Chief Officer, 2nd Eng, Hotel Mgr, Exec Chef ≈ €49k/month) with relief rotation: ≈ €0.75 m/yr.
- Seasonal crew (22) for about 7.5 months: ≈ €0.47 m.
- Base payroll ≈ **€1.2 m**. On-costs: travel/rotation flights, P&I crew cover is in P&I, training/STCW, uniforms, recruitment/manning agency, end-of-contract/leave pay: **+20–25%**.
- Social charges (see below): Malta/Madeira/non-resident crew ≈ 0–5%; Greek-flag crew under NAT ≈ 25–30%+.
- **All-in crew cost: €1.5–1.8 m/yr (international flag) and €1.8–2.2 m/yr (Greek flag with Greek manning)**. Crew victualling is under food.
- A cheaper "cruise-line" hotel staffing model (Filipino/Indonesian hotel crew on IBF-type scales) can cut hotel payroll by 30–40%, at a service-level risk for a luxury product.

**Employer social charges**
- **Greece (NAT)**: the employer-side components seen include supplementary insurance 3.5%, lump-sum fund 4.0%, family benefits 2.0% and seafarers' home 0.18%, on top of main pension and health contributions. A full current table was not captured. The general e-EFKA employer rate (non-seafarer) is **21.79%**. Greek-flag passenger ships also have minimum Greek/EU officer manning (composition decided by the Ministry). ESTIMATE total employer burden **~25–30%**, to validate with a Greek payroll adviser. Sources: https://repository-edulll.ekt.gr/edulll/retrieve/11404/3590_1.61_%ce%91%ce%9d_14_11_11.pdf ; https://rivermate.com/guides/greece/taxes
- **Malta flag**: social security is 10% of basic weekly wage, capped (2024: ~€52.23/week each for employer and employee). **Non-resident seafarers of a Maltese "shipping organisation" are exempt**. Non-EU crew can stay in their home-country system. CONFIRMED: https://gvzh.mt/insights/social-security-contributions-for-seafarers/ ; https://acrew.com/?p=165883 ; https://www.csbgroup.com/articles/malta-announces-updated-social-security-contribution-rates-for-2026/
- **Madeira (MAR)**: crew wages are **exempt from Portuguese income tax and social security**. CONFIRMED: https://mondaq.com/marine-shipping/741390/why-the-madeira-international-shipping-register-mar-is-so-attractive-and-the-advantages-of-registering-a-vessel-in-madeira
- EU crew: Reg. 883/2004 applies (flag state or residence/employer rules), so this needs case-by-case review.

**Consumption model, 60–68 m, 2 × ~1,400 kW mains** (ESTIMATE)
- Propulsion at 10–12 kn ≈ 50–60% MCR, about 1,300–1,600 kW → **~0.28–0.35 t/h**.
- Hotel load (HVAC, galley, laundry, desal) 250–400 kW on gensets → **~0.06–0.09 t/h, 24 h/day**.
- Greek island itinerary: typically **4–6 h underway/day** (40–60 nm hops), overnight in port or at anchor, with 1 sea day or longer leg per week.
- Daily: about 1.4–2.1 t propulsion + 1.4–2.2 t hotel = **~3–4 t/day; ~22–28 t per 7-night week**.
- Lay-up: about 0.5–1 t/day on shore power or a small genset.
- **PREMISE: 25 t/week × US$1,000/t base (≈ €870/t) ≈ €21.7k/week.** Stress case at US$1,500/t is about €32.6k/week, and at US$700/t about €15.2k/week. Fuel is roughly **€0.6–0.9 m/season** for 28 weeks.

## B. Regulation

| Topic | Finding | Label / Source |
|---|---|---|
| **>36 pax: SOLAS passenger ship** | The Red Ensign **Passenger Yacht Code (PYC)** covers yachts carrying **13–36 passengers** (≤200 persons total, international voyages) and is accepted as equivalent to SOLAS for passenger ships ≤36 pax. **At 72 pax the ship must be a full SOLAS passenger ship** (PSSC, SRtP/fire rules, ISM, ISPS, MLC). ## C. Corporate, flag and tax structure

| Jurisdiction | Key points | Label / Source |

**Recommended preliminary structure (PREMISE, for lawyers to validate)**
1. **QIMO Brasil (operator/seller)** → **EU HoldCo** (Portugal or Malta) → **Ship-owning SPV** (Malta Ltd, Malta flag, tonnage tax) **or** Madeira IBC with MAR flag.
2. A separate **operating/charterer company** (EU, possibly in Greece for local presence or Malta) bareboat- or time-charters the ship from the SPV and sells cruises B2B to QIMO and to third-party agents. This ring-fences PLR/consumer liabilities and makes it easier to finance or sell the SPV.
3. **Technical management** outsourced to an established Greek/Maltese/Cypriot passenger-ship manager (ISM DOC holder), since QIMO has no in-house DOC.
4. The EU flag secures cabotage in Greece, Croatia and Italy.
5. Brazilian CFC means the tax gain is mostly **cash-timing and credit planning**, not rate arbitrage. Choose the jurisdiction for **flag quality, financing and cabotage** first and tax second, and avoid jurisdictions on the IN 2.265/2025 list.
